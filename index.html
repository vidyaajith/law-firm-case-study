The Challenge

Stuck between five systems.

The firm's work was spread across five systems (Monday.com, Lawmatics, Dropbox, Zapier and Make), and none of them told the same story. The online team worked in one, the offline team worked in another, and status lived wherever someone last updated it by hand.

The before state: separate tools, no shared view

My Approach

Audit before automate.

Before building anything new, Monday.com and Lawmatics got audited on their own terms, then mapped against where online and offline work actually had to meet.

01

Audit Lawmatics

Traced every existing automation to find where it was silently failing.

02

Audit Monday.com

Mapped what the offline team actually needed visibility into, day to day.

03

Map the handoff

Traced the full path from lead to intake to case, online team to offline team.

04

Design the connection

Planned how Zapier and Make would keep every system in sync going forward.

The Gap

The firm wasn't short on effort, it was short on one system both teams could trust.

Audit Findings

Underneath it, 3 operational gaps.

  • Automations that misfired

    Lawmatics workflows were breaking silently, so the CRM data itself couldn't be trusted.

  • No shared view

    Neither Monday.com nor Lawmatics had a dashboard, so status lived in people's heads instead of on a screen.

  • Five systems, one business

    None of the systems talked to each other, so online and offline teams worked from two different pictures of the same firm.

The Build

6 moves, one shared system.

01

Fixed Lawmatics Automations

The system of record could finally be trusted.

The firm's Lawmatics automations were misfiring, silently breaking workflows the team depended on without anyone noticing until something fell through. Those were rebuilt from the ground up before anything else touched the system, so every automation downstream had a foundation that actually worked.

Lawmatics automation builder, before and after
02

Built Dashboards on Lawmatics

The case and client side became visible at a glance.

With the automations fixed, the CRM's data was finally trustworthy enough to build reporting on top of. New dashboards gave the online team, and leadership, a real-time view into where every case actually stood, instead of clicking through individual records to find out.

Lawmatics dashboard, live view
03

Built Automations on Monday.com

The offline team stopped chasing status by hand.

On the operations side, Monday.com automations took over the manual handoffs and status updates that used to depend on someone remembering to do them, so work moved forward without a person having to push it at every step.

  • Handoffs trigger themselves. Status changes now move a task to the next owner automatically.
  • Nothing waits on a reminder. The team stopped relying on someone remembering to update the board.
Monday.com automation recipe
04

Built Dashboards on Monday.com

The offline team got the same visibility the online team now had.

A matching set of dashboards went up on Monday.com, giving the operations side of the business the same real-time view Lawmatics now gave the case side, so both teams were finally looking at the same picture instead of two separate ones.

Monday.com dashboard, live view
05

Connected Everything, Documents Included

Online and offline finally ran off the same data.

Zapier and Make became the connective layer between Monday.com and Lawmatics, so an update in one system reflected in the other automatically. Dropbox, where all of the firm's documents lived, was wired into that same layer, so files stayed tied to the right case without anyone moving them by hand.

  • Real-time sync. Monday.com and Lawmatics stay in step with each other, no manual double-entry.
  • Documents connected. Dropbox, home to every case document, now ties directly into the same automation layer.
Make: syncing Dropbox and Monday.com
Zapier: routing Lawmatics into Dropbox
06

Set Up a Unified Weekly Meeting

The whole team stayed on the same priorities.

With every system finally showing the same picture, one weekly meeting was set up to check in on priorities. Online and offline teams now review where things stand together, which keeps everyone on track instead of drifting back into separate conversations.
